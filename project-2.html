NPFL Data Analytics

Analysing Geography, Population, Club Performance and League Competitiveness in the NPFL

A six-season football analytics project examining the relationship between geography, population and club performance in the Nigerian Premier Football League, while also exploring league competitiveness, home advantage, player contribution, stadium environments and match-outcome prediction.

THE CENTRAL QUESTION

Does population and geography explain football performance in the NPFL?

The analysis suggests that population size alone does not explain club performance. Instead, the strongest patterns emerge around sustained club performance, home advantage, competitive balance, player contribution and the broader sporting environment.

PROJECT OBJECTIVE

Moving beyond the league table

This project analyses six seasons of NPFL data from 2020/21 to 2025/26 to investigate the relationship between geography, population and football performance.

The analysis also evaluates league competitiveness, team and player performance, home advantage, stadium environments and the potential for match-outcome prediction.

The objective was not simply to describe what happened in the league, but to investigate the patterns behind performance and identify what the findings could imply for the wider NPFL ecosystem.

Stadium & Match Environment

Predictive Modelling

SQL SERVER

Building the analytical warehouse

SQL Server was used to transform the source datasets into a structured analytical warehouse.

The warehouse follows a dimensional modelling approach with three main schemas:

stg Staging
dim Dimensions
fact Facts

Dimension tables include states, seasons, players, dates, stadiums and teams.

Fact tables include matches, player statistics and team statistics.

KEY FINDINGS

What the data revealed

01

Population Does Not Explain Club Performance

The analysis did not find statistically significant evidence that clubs from more populated states consistently achieve higher performance.

Population may provide a larger potential talent and supporter base, but the analysis suggests that population by itself does not determine sporting success.

02

Geography Matters, But There Is No Simple North-South Divide

Club performance varied across Nigeria's geopolitical zones, with the strongest average club PPG observed in the southern zones.

1.45 South South
1.42 South West
1.42 South East
1.15 North East
03

NPFL Competitiveness Improved

Outside the abridged 2022/23 season, later seasons showed a more compressed competitive distribution.

39% approximate reduction in PPG spread from 2020/21 to 2025/26
04

Sustained Success Is Concentrated

Several clubs repeatedly appeared among the strongest performers across the six-season period.

Rivers United · Enyimba · Enugu Rangers · Plateau United · Remo Stars · Kano Pillars · Abia Warriors

Rivers United recorded the highest overall points total in the project's six-season club analysis.

05

Home Advantage Is a Major NPFL Characteristic

2.18 Home PPG
0.56 Away PPG

Home teams averaged almost four times as many points per game as away teams.

Home Win 63.68% Draw 24.47% Away Win 11.84%
06

Player Goal Contribution Is Concentrated Among Forwards

In the 2025/26 player analysis, forwards accounted for almost two-thirds of recorded goal contribution.

Forward 63.72%
Midfielder 21.48%
Defender 14.80%
07

xG Efficiency Provides Useful Context

Some clubs recorded substantially more goals than their modelled expected goals, while others recorded less.

The highest actual-goals-to-xG ratios included El-Kanemi Warriors, Kano Pillars, Katsina United, Enugu Rangers and Kun Khalifat.

Actual goals versus xG is treated as an efficiency indicator rather than definitive proof of finishing skill.

08

Stadiums Are Part of the League Product

Stadium analysis examined match usage, capacity, average goals and home/away performance.

Among the most-used venues were Enyimba International Stadium, Godswill Akpabio International Stadium, Pantami Stadium, New Jos Stadium and Nnamdi Azikiwe Stadium.

09

Predictive Modelling Shows Strong Home-Win Recognition

64.9% approximate overall accuracy

The Random Forest model demonstrated useful predictive information in historical team-performance variables.

However, substantial class imbalance meant that the model strongly favoured home wins and performed poorly when identifying draws and away wins.

DATA QUALITY & LIMITATIONS

Reading the analysis with context

Abridged 2022/23 Season

The 2022/23 season contains fewer matches, making direct comparison of some season-level totals inappropriate without considering season structure.

Duplicate Player Records

Duplicate player records were identified during preparation and handled where appropriate.

Missing Stadium Information

Some match records contain missing or unknown stadium information.

Geographic Mapping

Geographic analysis depends on the quality and consistency of state and club mapping.

Advanced Player Statistics

Some advanced player statistics are incomplete across the full analysis period.

xG Model Limitations

Expected-goals metrics depend on the underlying model and should be interpreted as modelled estimates.

Predictive Model Class Imbalance

Overall accuracy alone is not an adequate measure of model quality because the outcome classes were substantially imbalanced.

Causality

The project is primarily observational. Identified relationships should not automatically be interpreted as causal relationships.

OVERALL CONCLUSION

Population isn't the limiting factor.

The analysis produces a more nuanced picture of the NPFL than a simple population-versus-performance comparison.

Nigeria has a large population, deep football culture and a strong history of football achievement. However, the analysis does not show population size alone translating directly into club performance.

Instead, the strongest patterns are found in sustained club performance, home advantage, competitive balance, player contribution, geographic differences, stadium environments, data availability and sporting systems.

The reduction in PPG variation across the six-season period suggests that the league became more competitively balanced toward 2025/26, while repeated high performance among certain clubs shows that sustained success remains achievable for clubs capable of maintaining strong systems.

Nigeria has the football talent and culture; the data suggests population isn't the limiting factor — the bigger opportunity is building the sporting, infrastructure, data, broadcast and commercial systems that convert that potential into sustained domestic value.

DATA SOURCE & LICENSING

About the underlying data

The football datasets used in this project were obtained through a paid FootyStats subscription.

The underlying source datasets are not included in the public repository. Original CSV files, cleaned versions that substantially reproduce the licensed source data, database backups and full database dumps are intentionally excluded.

The project instead focuses on demonstrating analytical methodology, SQL engineering, statistical analysis, data modelling, Power BI development, DAX calculations, visualisation, analytical storytelling, findings and recommendations.
