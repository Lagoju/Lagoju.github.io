01 · OVERVIEW

Does population explain football performance?

The Nigerian Premier Football League (NPFL) is the highest level of domestic professional football in Nigeria.

This project analyses six seasons of NPFL data from 2020/21 to 2025/26 to investigate the relationship between geography, population, club performance, league competitiveness, player contribution and stadium environments.

The central question was straightforward: does population and geography explain football performance in the NPFL, or are other sporting and structural factors more important?

THE CENTRAL QUESTION

Bigger population ≠ automatically better football performance.

The analysis found no statistically significant relationship between population and club performance in the sample.

07 · STATISTICAL EVIDENCE

Population vs club performance

The relationship between population and club performance was tested using both Pearson and Spearman correlation.

Pearson correlation r = 0.124 p = 0.5548
Spearman correlation ρ = 0.0546 p = 0.7954
RESULT

No statistically significant relationship

Population may provide a talent pool, supporter base or commercial opportunity, but it did not explain club performance in this sample.

08 · KEY FINDINGS

What the analysis revealed

01

Population Does Not Explain Club Performance

The relationship between population and club performance was weak and statistically insignificant. Other sporting and structural factors appear more important.

02

03

NPFL Competitiveness Improved

PPG spread fell from 1.21 in 2020/21 to 0.74 in 2025/26 — approximately a 39% reduction.

04

Sustained Success Is Concentrated

Rivers United, Enyimba, Enugu Rangers, Plateau United, Remo Stars, Kano Pillars and Abia Warriors repeatedly appeared among the stronger-performing clubs.

05

Home Advantage Is Major

2.18 Home PPG
vs
0.56 Away PPG

Home teams won 63.68% of matches, compared with 11.84% away wins.

06

Player Contribution Is Concentrated

In 2025/26, forwards accounted for 63.72% of recorded goal contribution, followed by midfielders at 21.48% and defenders at 14.80%.

07

xG Efficiency Varies

El-Kanemi Warriors, Kano Pillars, Katsina United, Enugu Rangers and Kun Khalifat recorded some of the highest actual-goals-to-xG ratios.

08

Stadiums Matter to the Matchday Product

Enyimba International Stadium, Godswill Akpabio International Stadium, Pantami Stadium, New Jos Stadium and Nnamdi Azikiwe Stadium were among the most frequently used venues.

09

Predictive Modelling Shows Promise — With Caveats

The Random Forest model achieved approximately 64.9% accuracy, with stronger recognition of home wins than draws or away wins due partly to class imbalance.

12 · LIMITATIONS

What the analysis cannot tell us

2022/23 was an abridged season.
Some player records contained duplicates.
Some stadium information was missing or unknown.
Geographic mapping quality varied across records.
Advanced player statistics were incomplete in some cases.
xG analysis depends on the underlying xG model.
Predictive modelling was affected by class imbalance.
Observational analysis does not establish causality.

14 · CONCLUSION

Population is only part of the story.

The NPFL analysis produced a more nuanced picture than a simple population-performance relationship.

The strongest patterns appeared around sustained club performance, home advantage, competitive balance, player contribution, geographic differences, stadium environments and the availability of football data.

The reduction in PPG variation suggests a more balanced league by 2025/26, while repeated high performance among certain clubs highlights the importance of sustainable sporting systems.

Nigeria already has the football talent and culture. The bigger opportunity is building the sporting, infrastructure, data, broadcast and commercial systems that can consistently convert that potential into value.
