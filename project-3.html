PROJECT 03 · DATA ANALYTICS · PYTHON · POWER BI

Nigeria Presidential Election Analysis (2015–2023)

How has voting behaviour in Nigeria changed across three presidential elections? This project combines national, state-level and 2023 polling-unit results to examine voter participation, party competition, electoral volatility and changing political patterns.

01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

The question behind the analysis

I started with a simple question: how has voting behaviour in Nigeria changed between the 2015, 2019 and 2023 presidential elections? Answering it required more than comparing national vote totals. I examined participation, party vote shares, winning margins, state-level winner changes and the movement of votes between parties.

The project combines harmonised national and state-level election results with a separate dataset of 123,918 cross-checked polling-unit records from the 2023 election. Python was used for data preparation, statistical analysis and clustering, while Power BI was used to communicate the findings through an interactive report.

The analysis describes patterns in the available election data. It does not establish why individual voters made particular choices, and associations are not treated as proof of causation.

02 / RESEARCH OBJECTIVES

What I set out to investigate

The broad idea of voting behaviour was broken into measurable questions.

01

Voter participation

How did registration, accreditation, turnout and rejected ballots change across the three elections?

02

Party competition

Did APC and PDP remain dominant, or did 2023 reveal a more fragmented party landscape?

03

State-level changes

Which states changed their winning party, and how did party vote shares and winning margins move?

04

Electoral volatility

Were states that changed winners also characterised by greater movement in party vote shares?

05

Geographic patterns

How did party support and turnout vary across Nigeria's geopolitical zones?

06

Polling units and clusters

What can the available 2023 polling-unit data reveal, and can states be grouped by similar electoral changes?

03 / KEY FINDINGS

What the data revealed

Selected descriptive findings from the compiled datasets, subject to the source and coverage limitations documented in the project.

FINDING 01

A growing electorate, but declining turnout

Registered voters increased from 67.4 million in 2015 to approximately 93.2 million in 2023. Turnout fell from 47.09% to 27.13%.

−19.96 pp turnout change, 2015–2023
FINDING 02

Party competition became more fragmented

In the comparable APC/PDP/Other grouping, the combined APC and PDP vote share fell to 65.69% in 2023. LP and NNPP were substantial forces in the separate full-party breakdown.

2.33 pp 2023 comparable winning margin
FINDING 03

State-level winner switching increased

25 of 37 states/reporting units changed their winning party between 2019 and 2023, compared with 6 of 37 between 2015 and 2019.

67.6% changed winner, 2019–2023
FINDING 04

Winner-changing states had higher mean volatility

States that changed winners had mean volatility of 48.80, compared with 22.44 for states that did not switch winners.

48.80 vs 22.44 mean volatility, 2019–2023
FINDING 05

Polling-unit results add another layer

Across 123,918 cross-checked records, APC recorded 50,797 polling-unit wins, LP 40,052, PDP 26,849 and NNPP 6,220.

32 of 37 polling-unit winner matched state winner
FINDING 06

States showed different patterns of change

K-means clustering grouped the 37 reporting units into six clusters using standardised changes in APC share, PDP share and comparable ENP between 2019 and 2023.

6 clusters descriptive state groupings

Important: APC/PDP/Other is used for longitudinal comparison. The full 2023 party breakdown is presented separately; the two groupings are not interchangeable.

06 / DATA QUALITY & LIMITATIONS

What the analysis cannot tell us

Being transparent about the data is part of the analysis, not an afterthought.

Source comparability

The election datasets differ in structure and available fields. Harmonisation improves comparison but cannot remove every source-definition issue.

National figure discrepancies

Some working figures differ from INEC references. These differences are documented and should be reconciled before official use.

Polling-unit coverage

The 123,918 records have uneven coverage across states and are not a complete, uniformly representative national census.

Data-quality flags

7,951 records were flagged because combined votes for the four named parties exceeded accredited voters. A flag requires investigation; it does not establish fraud.

Regression sample size

The models use only 37 state/FCT observations. Results are exploratory and may be sensitive to individual states and model choices.

Ecological inference

State-level patterns cannot establish individual voter motivations. The analysis does not make causal claims.

A note on polling-unit coverage: the project records a range from approximately 1.98% in Zamfara to 91.90% in Ekiti for the source classification used in its coverage check. This describes the share of records cross-checked under that classification; it is not proof that other polling units did not exist or report results.

07 / CONCLUSION

A changing electoral landscape

Across 2015–2023, the compiled data show a larger registered electorate alongside declining accreditation and turnout. The 2023 presidential election also shows a more fragmented party landscape, while state-level winner switching increased sharply between 2019 and 2023. States that changed winners recorded higher mean electoral volatility in this dataset.

These results describe changes in recorded election outcomes. Explaining why those changes occurred would require additional evidence, including voter-level surveys, campaign and candidate information, local context and more complete election-administration data.
